A shift in leverage

Leadership growth is less about adding skills and more about changing where your leverage comes from.

A useful self-check

Where does your week go?

The Leadership Spectrum runs from doing the work yourself to helping others find their own answers. Most of us sit closer to Do and Tell than we’d like. Growth shifts the balance toward teaching, advising and coaching.

And leadership has an altitude: Surface → Structure → Strategy → Vision. If a VP spends the week at Surface, the Vision work isn’t getting done.

Three places context tends to leak

01 / Handoffs

Tool-to-tool jumping

ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Claude, Figma Make: only part of the context makes each trip.

02 / Compression

Prompts get shorter

Detailed personas and journeys become brief prompts. The nuance disappears.

03 / Shortcuts

Direct jump to code

Screens arrive fast without the user, journey or outcome that makes them meaningful.

The delivery shift

Code first.
Refine with intent.

AI can make a reviewable baseline cheap and visible early. Designers can then test the flow, improve accessibility, align to the design system, and govern what ships.

A five-day sprint now has a working MVP on Thursday—and user review on Friday. Speed gives the team more to learn from; it doesn’t decide what is good.

The context chain

Every stage adds.
Nothing replaces.

One shared source of context feeds the whole delivery chain. The accelerator framework moves from intent to persona, journey, stories, information architecture and a first-cut React UI—while keeping earlier decisions available.

In the hands-on Context Drift Relay, teams passed work between roles using a shared sheet: what must everyone remember, whatever they’re working on?

04   /   Back to the team

Small practices.
Better defaults.

People & team

  • Run a Team Health Assessment; act on the three lowest scores.
  • Map team levels and check whether the shape is becoming top-heavy.
  • Add an AI skill family to career pathways, including working with agents.
  • Ask each person how they prefer to be recognised.

Strategy & influence

  • Connect design capacity to the priorities leadership has set.
  • Build a one-page view of investment, coverage and gaps.
  • Set a deliberate one-on-one rhythm with product and engineering peers.
  • Make design’s business impact visible throughout the year.

AI-assisted delivery

  • Pilot a Shared Context Sheet on the next AI-assisted initiative.
  • Keep one source of context for every person and tool.
  • Use “code first, refine later” with human checkpoints.
  • Check accessibility, design-system fit and release decisions.
